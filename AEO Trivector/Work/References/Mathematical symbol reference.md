## Symbol definitions (plain language)

- ∈: “is an element of”; $x ∈ A$ means $x$ belongs to set $A$.
- ⊂, ⊆: “subset”; $A ⊂ B$ often strict, $A ⊆ B$ allows equality.
- ∩, ∪: intersection and union of sets.
- ⇒, ⇔: logical implication and equivalence between propositions.
- ∧, ∨, ¬: logical and, or, not.
- ∑, ∏: sum and product over an index set.
- ∂: partial derivative; $\partial f / \partial x$ is derivative w.r.t. $x$.
- ∇: gradient, divergence, or Laplacian depending on context.
- ⊕, ⊗: direct sum and tensor product of spaces/objects.
- ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ, ℂ: natural, integer, rational, real, complex numbers.
- ⊢: syntactic entailment (provability in a formal system).
- ⊨: semantic entailment (truth in all models).
- ℘: power set, the set of all subsets.
- ↪, ↠: injective (mono) and surjective (epi) maps.
- ≅: isomorphism (invertible structure‑preserving map).
- ≃: equivalence (often up to homotopy or higher structure).
These are standard across modern mathematics, logic, and category theory.[^1][^2]


## Greek / Latin in math (role‑based)

Some especially common roles for Greek and Latin symbols:

- α, β, γ, δ: generic scalars/parameters; δ also for small perturbations.
- ε: error tolerance or small positive real ($\varepsilon\to 0$).
- λ: eigenvalues, Lagrange multipliers, rates (Poisson parameter).
- μ: measures, means, or friction/viscosity coefficients.
- ν: frequency or measures.
- π: circle constant; also projections $\pi_1, \pi_2$.
- ρ: density (mass, probability), correlation.
- σ: standard deviation; also $σ$-algebras in measure theory.
- τ: time constants, topology functors, or delay.
- φ, ψ: generic functions, wavefunctions in quantum mechanics.
- ω: angular frequency, differential forms ($\omega$), or final object index.
- A, B, C, …: sets, algebras, objects in a category.
- f, g, h: generic functions or morphisms.
- x, y, z: points, coordinates, variables.
